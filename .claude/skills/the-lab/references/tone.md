# Tone and content rules

## Register

Write the way a good handover sounds: direct, specific, unhurried, no
performance. The reader is a working nurse who has fifteen minutes. Respect
that by being clear, not by being brief in a way that costs the meaning.

Short declaratives. Prose over bullets in the case narrative — the case is a
story with a sequence, and bullets destroy sequence. Bullets are fine for
observations and investigations, where the reader is scanning for a number.

## Australian English, always

Organise, recognise, colour, honour, practise (verb) and practice (noun),
oedema, haemodynamic, anaesthetic, paediatric, dyspnoea, litre, metre,
programme only where it means a broadcast. Millimoles per litre. Twenty-four
hour time written as a duration where possible.

## Banned

- Exclamation marks. All of them.
- "Exciting", "thrilled", "delighted", "amazing", "incredible", "journey",
  "empower", "unleash", "game-changing", "revolutionise", "cutting-edge",
  "seamless", "deep dive", "unpack", "at the end of the day".
- Second-person marketing address: "you'll love", "you won't believe".
- Rhetorical questions used as headings.
- Emoji.
- Any sentence whose function is to say the newsletter is good.

## The evidence rule

**Never state a clinical fact the source case does not support.** Not
"probably", not "would typically", not filling a gap with what usually
happens. If the submission does not say what the lactate was, the issue does
not say what the lactate was — it says `[NOT SUPPLIED]` and the reviewer
decides whether to chase it.

**No efficacy, mortality, incidence or outcome percentage without a citation
to a published source.** No exceptions, including figures that appear in
public coverage or in the contributor's own submission. A number the
contributor remembers is not a source.

Where the submission is ambiguous — two readings of the same sentence, an
observation that could be a typo — flag it for the reviewer inline as
`[AMBIGUOUS: ...]` rather than choosing. Choosing is a clinical act and it is
not yours.

## CPD wording

CPD hours in this program are **self-declared**. You may write how long an
activity takes:

> This issue takes about forty minutes to read and complete.

You may not write, imply, or paraphrase:

> earns one CPD hour · accredited · endorsed · counts towards your
> registration · APHRA-approved · CPD points

There is no wording here you are permitted to improvise. If a draft needs to
say something about CPD beyond duration, mark it `[BRAD]` and leave it.

## Credit

The contributor is named, with role and unit, in every issue their case
appears in. The unit bio runs with it — what this unit sees, who walks
through the door. That bio is the reason the program reads as peer-led rather
than corporate, so it is not optional and it is not generic.

## Naming the program and its sites

The Lab is independent. It is not "the hospital's program", not branded to a
group, and copy should never imply endorsement by a hospital, a group, or a
regulator that has not given it in writing.

**Site names are business-sensitive and this repository is public.** Keep
them out of tracked files — refer to sites by descriptor ("the coastal
private site", "site A") in anything committed. The roster of participating
sites lives in an untracked local file. Same rule as
`docs/constraints.local.md`; same reason.

## Length

- Case narrative: 300–500 words.
- Teaching point: three sentences, hard limit.
- Paper summary: one paragraph, practice-focused — what it changes on shift,
  not what the authors did.
- Drug or device note: 100–150 words.
- Contributor bio: two to three sentences.
- Unit bio: two to three sentences.
