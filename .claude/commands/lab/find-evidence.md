Load the `the-lab` skill, then read `references/tone.md` for the evidence
rule.

Find evidence for: $ARGUMENTS

## Steps

1. **Read `structured.md`** and identify the clinical question the case
   actually poses — the decision the nurse had to make, not the diagnosis.
   That question is what the paper has to speak to. A paper about the
   condition that says nothing about the decision is the wrong paper.

2. **Search PubMed.** Prefer, in order: guidelines from a recognised body,
   systematic reviews and meta-analyses, then large primary studies. Prefer
   the last ten years unless the seminal paper is older and still the
   reference point. Prefer nursing-relevant and Australian-relevant sources
   where the quality is comparable.

3. **Verify every citation against the record.** Authors, title, journal,
   year, volume, pages, DOI or PMID, checked — not reconstructed from memory.
   **A citation you cannot verify does not go in the draft.** State plainly
   that you could not verify it rather than producing a plausible one; a
   fabricated citation in a clinical newsletter is the fastest way to end
   this program's credibility.

4. **Propose two to three candidates**, one marked preferred, each with the
   full citation, the study type, the population, and one line on why it
   is or is not the best fit for this case.

5. **Write one paragraph on the preferred paper**, practice-focused: what it
   changes on shift, not what the authors did. Australian English, clinical
   register.

6. **Any figure needs its citation attached** — efficacy, mortality,
   incidence, effect size, number needed to treat. No exceptions, including
   figures that appear in public coverage. Where the paper's finding cannot
   be stated without a number you cannot source, say what the paper found
   qualitatively and mark the number `[NEEDS SOURCE]`.

7. **Note the access position** — open access, paywalled, or free full text
   via PubMed Central — so the issue can say honestly whether a reader can
   follow the link.

## Report

The candidates with verified citations, the preferred one and why, the
practice paragraph, any citation you could not verify, and any claim you had
to leave qualitative for want of a source.
