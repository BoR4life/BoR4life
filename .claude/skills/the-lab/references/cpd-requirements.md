# What CPD actually requires in Australia

Regulatory facts, so the agent stops at what is true rather than what other
platforms imply. Sources are cited because everything here is checkable and
none of it may be paraphrased into a claim.

## The requirement

The Nursing and Midwifery Board of Australia requires a registered nurse to
complete **a minimum of 20 hours of continuing professional development per
registration period**, which runs **1 June to 31 May**. Records must be kept
for **five years**. Evidence is produced only if the NMBA selects the
practitioner for audit, and the practitioner then has a limited window to
respond.

Source: NMBA, *Guidelines: Continuing professional development* —
https://www.nursingmidwiferyboard.gov.au/Codes-Guidelines-Statements/Codes-Guidelines/Guidelines-cpd.aspx

## The thing almost everyone gets wrong

**Nursing CPD in Australia has no accreditation gatekeeper.** There is no body
that pre-approves a nursing CPD activity the way medical colleges accredit
activities for their fellows. Self-directed learning counts in full, provided
it is relevant to the practitioner's practice and documented.

Two consequences:

1. **"Accredited" is a marketing word in this market, not a regulatory
   status.** The Lab must never use it, and the fact that competitors do is
   not a reason to.
2. **The hour is not the product.** Anyone can count hours. What the NMBA
   asks for at audit is harder to produce and almost nobody supplies it.

## What audit evidence must actually show

Not a certificate. Not an hours tally. The evidence has to demonstrate that
the practitioner:

- identified and prioritised their learning needs, based on self-reflection
  and evaluation of their own practice;
- developed a learning plan from those needs;
- participated in learning activities that address them;
- **reflected on the value of those activities to their practice.**

That is a reflective artefact tied to a specific gap in a specific
practitioner's practice. A completion certificate does not meet it. This is
the single most useful fact in this file.

## What it means for The Lab

A quiz that scores a decision knows two things a video library never does:
**which decision this nurse got wrong, and why the distractor was
attractive.** That is exactly the raw material of a reflection statement —
the learning need is identified by the wrong answer, not self-nominated from
a blank page, which is the part nurses find hardest and leave until audit.

So the issue's output for a participant is not a certificate. It is a
**draft reflection** they can edit and keep:

> This month I worked through a case of fast atrial fibrillation with
> haemodynamic compromise. I selected rate control for an unstable patient.
> The learning was that instability moves the patient out of the rate-control
> pathway altogether. In my own practice I will state the blood pressure and
> the perfusion out loud before drawing up a rate-controlling drug, and I
> will name instability as the reason when I escalate.

Four sentences: activity, identified gap, what changed, how it applies. That
is the audit shape.

**Rules that still bind absolutely.** The draft is the participant's record,
not The Lab's assertion. The agent may state the duration of the activity. It
may never state or imply hours earned, accreditation, endorsement, points, or
that completing an issue satisfies any part of a registration requirement.
The participant decides what to claim and self-declares it. Wording beyond
duration is `[BRAD]`, never improvised.

## Duration wording that is permitted

> CPD hours in The Lab are self-declared. Record the time you spend against
> your own CPD plan.

Nothing further.
