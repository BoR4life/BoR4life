# SAMPLE — FABRICATED. Not a real patient, contributor, or unit.

Test fixture for the de-identification stage. Every person, number, place and
event below is invented. Written as continuous prose to mimic a submission
typed straight into an email body.

---

Sending this one through for the imaging strand. Patient was R.T., a 34 year
old man, admitted 22 September 2026 to Rivermouth District Hospital, which as
you know is the only place within ninety minutes that takes trauma. He works
as the local fire captain so half the unit recognised him on arrival. He came
off a trail bike at speed, helmeted, no LOC. Initial obs were HR 112, BP
104/68, RR 26, SpO2 96% on 6L Hudson, GCS 15.

The team's first read was simple rib fractures. CXR in resus showed left-sided
rib fractures 4 to 7 and what the RMO called "a bit of contusion". Dr Ansari
looked at it twenty minutes later and asked why the left hemidiaphragm was so
high. We went to CT. It was a diaphragmatic rupture with stomach in the left
chest — genuinely the first case any of us had seen at this hospital.

He went to theatre 3 that night, laparotomy and repair, ICU two days,
discharged day nine. His address is 41 Kingfisher Crescent, Rivermouth QLD
4573 if anyone needs to follow up, and his mobile is 0455 218 664.

The point I want to make is that a CXR in a trauma patient is not a rib film.
The abnormality was on the first image and nobody looked past the fractures.
Satisfaction of search is the whole lesson.
