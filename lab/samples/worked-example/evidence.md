# Evidence — 2026-10-rhythm-01

**Worked example, built from a fabricated case. Citations below are real and
were verified against PubMed on 6 Sep 2026.**

## The clinical question the case poses

Not "what is atrial fibrillation" but: **when a patient in fast AF is
haemodynamically compromised, what changes about the pathway, and what is the
nurse's part in recognising that it has changed?** A paper about AF that does
not speak to the instability branch is the wrong paper for this issue.

## Candidates

### Preferred

**Joglar JA, Chung MK, Armbruster AL, et al. 2023 ACC/AHA/ACCP/HRS Guideline
for the Diagnosis and Management of Atrial Fibrillation: A Report of the
American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association Joint Committee on
Clinical Practice Guidelines. Circulation. 2024;149(1):e1–e156.**
doi:10.1161/CIR.0000000000001193 · PMID 38033089 · free full text via PubMed
Central.

Practice guideline. The current comprehensive statement on AF diagnosis and
management, and the one that sets out the separation between the rate-control
pathway and immediate cardioversion for the patient who is not tolerating the
rhythm. Chosen because it speaks directly to the branch point this case turned
on, and because it is the document the registrar and the nurse in this story
were both, implicitly, arguing about.

### Alternates

**Pluymaekers NAHA, Dudink EAMP, Luermans JGLM, et al. Early or Delayed
Cardioversion in Recent-Onset Atrial Fibrillation. N Engl J Med.
2019;380(16):1499–1508.** doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1900353 · PMID 30883054.

RACE 7 ACWAS. Randomised, 437 patients, and the reason "wait and see" entered
the conversation about recent-onset AF. Deliberately **not** the preferred
paper: it enrolled haemodynamically *stable* patients only, which makes it the
most instructive near-miss in the file. It is the trial a reader will reach
for, and it does not apply to this patient. Worth running as an alternate for
exactly that reason.

**Brieger D, Amerena J, Attia J, et al. National Heart Foundation of Australia
and the Cardiac Society of Australia and New Zealand: Australian Clinical
Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Management of Atrial Fibrillation 2018.
Heart Lung Circ. 2018;27(10):1209–1266.** doi:10.1016/j.hlc.2018.06.1043 ·
PMID 30077228.

The Australian guideline, and therefore the one closest to the practice
environment these readers work in. Listed as an alternate rather than
preferred only because it is the older document; where it and the 2023
guideline differ, local policy governs and the difference is worth a line in
the issue.

## Practice paragraph — preferred paper

Draft for review:

> Most fast AF is a rate problem, and most of the time the pathway is a
> rate-controlling drug and a look for the trigger. The guideline's value at
> the bedside is that it makes explicit where that pathway stops: when the
> patient is not tolerating the rhythm — hypotensive, poorly perfused,
> ischaemic, in pulmonary oedema — the question is no longer which drug but
> how soon the rhythm can be restored. For the nurse, that matters because
> the finding which moves a patient across that line is usually a bedside
> one, and it is usually available before the next observation set. Knowing
> the branch exists is what turns "her BP is 88 over 54" from an
> observation into an argument.

## Reviewer notes

- The paragraph above is written at the level the guideline's scope and
  abstract support. **The specific recommendation and its class of
  recommendation have not been read from the full text.** Before publication,
  a reviewer should confirm the wording against the guideline's section on
  acute management and instability, and either cite the recommendation
  precisely or leave the paragraph as it stands.
- No effect size, mortality figure or percentage is quoted anywhere in this
  draft. Nothing here needs one, and none may be added without its citation.
- Access: the preferred guideline is free full text via PubMed Central. The
  NEJM trial and the Australian guideline are paywalled for most readers, so
  the issue should not imply that a reader can simply click through.
