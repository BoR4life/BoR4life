## The company

Immersive learning for healthcare. Founded 2018 by Brad Chesham — a nurse who
worked across Australia, the UK, Afghanistan and Iraq, and saw that
conventional training does not prepare people for high-consequence moments.
Based in Buderim, Queensland.

The line that matters: **built by nurses, for nurses.** That is the moat. Every
competitor in this space is a software company that hired a clinical advisor.
Bundle of Rays is the inverse, and the site should never let a visitor forget it.

## Audience — and why this changes the design

Three buyers, in order of deal value:

1. **Institutional / government procurement** (health departments, national
   strategies). Needs: evidence, accreditation, security and data handling,
   deployment scale, references, procurement documentation. Reads on desktop,
   often on a locked-down browser. **Low tolerance for a site that makes them
   hunt.**
2. **Academic decision-makers** (deans, curriculum leads). Needs: pedagogical
   grounding, learning outcomes, curriculum fit, integration with the LMS.
3. **Clinical educators and end users**. Needs: what it actually feels like,
   what modules exist, hardware requirements.

All three are *evaluating credibility*, not browsing for delight. Delight is
how you earn the right to be evaluated — it is not the conversion mechanism.

## Positioning

> Bundle of Rays builds clinically authored immersive training that measurably
> changes what practitioners do under pressure.

Three pillars to structure the whole site around:

- **Clinically authored** — nurses wrote it, not a studio guessing.
- **Measurably effective** — analytics capture decisions, communication,
  procedural accuracy. Bring the numbers forward.
- **Deployed at national scale** — this is not a pilot vendor.

## Voice

Calm, precise, clinical. Short declaratives. The subject matter is
life-and-death; the copy should carry that weight without melodrama. No
"revolutionise", no "unleash", no "game-changing". Let the client list and the
data create the drama.

Write like a discharge summary that happens to be persuasive: every claim
specific, attributable and falsifiable.
